## Narrow job tested

Given one address for an older home, produce a beautiful, source-linked “house biography” containing:

- historic photographs and maps;
- construction and neighborhood context;
- prior occupants and their occupations when lawfully public;
- verified changes in address, footprint or use;
- a dated timeline with confidence labels and explicit gaps;
- a print-ready keepsake.

The target buyer was a homeowner, gift buyer or real-estate professional seeking emotional connection and factual provenance rather than valuation, title assurance or legal due diligence.

## Public-data feasibility

Useful lawful sources exist, but coverage is jurisdiction-specific:

- NYC Municipal Archives provides roughly 720,000 tax photographs from the 1939–1941 program and address/block-lot search paths.
- Library of Congress and other repositories provide Sanborn fire-insurance maps and historic collections.
- Local assessor, deed, permit, city-directory, newspaper and census access varies by city, date and archive.
- UK National Archives guidance states that local archives are the primary place to search ownership and construction history.
- South Australia Archives notes that records and plans are incomplete and that plans of private homes before the 1950s are rare.
- Address changes, renumbering, parcel splits and missing digitization require interpretation; deeper sources often require paid subscriptions, local-library access or direct archive contact.

Sources:

- https://nycrecords.access.preservica.com/1940s-tax-photographs/
- https://www.archives.nyc/
- https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/help-with-your-research/research-guides/houses/
- https://archives.sa.gov.au/finding-information/discover-our-collection/house-and-land/house-or-property-history
- https://www.architecturaldigest.com/story/property-history-how-to-look-up-the-history-of-your-home

A public landmark fixture would overstate capability because famous properties have atypically rich documentation. An ordinary-address fixture in one well-digitized city could prove only that locality, not a general product.

## Exact substitution

The proposed distinction—automated, address-first, sourced history presented as a keepsake—is already occupied:

- HouseNovel offers a free collaborative home-history platform plus paid public-record research and an automated home-history report.
- Housetory accepts an address, builds a timeline from archives and records, identifies style/era, and offers poster/reel keepsakes.
- Houstorical markets an address-to-live-report and print-ready PDF cross-referenced from primary public records.
- Household History advertises concise two-page mini histories with past owners, historical maps and newspaper mentions.
- House History Check and several property-report products advertise flat-price address-history reports.
- Findmypast provides address/street house-history search over census and related records in the UK.

Generic property reports also cover permits, sales, hazards, ownership and valuation. They are not identical to an emotional biography, but they remove much of the address-resolution and public-record aggregation value.

## Competitive conclusion

Neither “AI,” source citations, address-first lookup, historic photos, a timeline, confidence labels nor a print-ready keepsake is sufficient differentiation.

The residual high-value work is precisely what resists near-zero operation:

- resolving historical address and parcel changes;
- inspecting undigitized or image-only archives;
- contacting local societies or building departments;
- interpreting contradictory deeds, maps and census records;
- distinguishing an occupant from an owner;
- handling living-person privacy and sensitive events;
- writing a coherent narrative without inventing gaps.

Current paid incumbents explicitly use human researchers for that layer. Automating only the easy public-data layer would compete with emerging exact products and free local archives.

## Decision

Close before build.

Do not create:

- another address-history lookup;
- a generic property timeline;
- a historic-photo wrapper;
- a mock house-biography PDF;
- a public-landmark demo;
- a property-risk or title-assurance report;
- a crawler that bypasses archive terms or paid access.

A synthetic or famous-house proof would answer technical formatting, not the missing commercial predicate.

## Re-entry criteria

Re-enter only if all are true:

1. one specific jurisdiction exposes lawful, stable, machine-readable sources covering at least historic image, parcel/build date, permits and neighborhood context;
2. a representative sample of ordinary pre-1950 addresses shows materially higher factual coverage than HouseNovel, Housetory, Houstorical and free official tools;
3. address-change and parcel ambiguity can fail closed without routine researcher intervention;
4. the output excludes title, valuation, legal, safety and historic-designation assurance;
5. living-person and sensitive-event data have a narrow privacy policy and minimization rule;
6. a repeatable, lawful unattended acquisition path is evidenced;
7. external WTT/WTP is obtained only after separate outreach/publication authority.

## Kill criteria

Reject immediately if:

- the product works only for famous or registered properties;
- depth depends on paid genealogy/news subscriptions or contacting local archives;
- ordinary addresses routinely require manual parcel/address reconciliation;
- the output silently infers occupants, ownership, causes of alterations or sensitive events;
- differentiation is only AI, citations, timeline, PDF, historic photo or gift presentation;
- coverage cannot be measured against an exact incumbent on the same addresses.
