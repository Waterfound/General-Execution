## Narrow lawful job tested

Potential buyer: a legitimate business, nonprofit or professional practice whose ordinary customer, staff or transactional email is bouncing or reaching spam.

Candidate autonomous artifact: given a public domain and an optional consented email-header sample, produce a read-only, source-pinned report covering:

- SPF syntax, multiplicity, DNS lookup pressure and visible authorization;
- published DKIM selectors when discoverable, with explicit incompleteness;
- DMARC record, alignment policy and reporting destinations;
- MX, MTA-STS, TLS-RPT and BIMI presence where applicable;
- public blocklist/reputation signals;
- exact observed records, uncertainties and provider-specific next checks;
- no DNS mutation and no promise of inbox placement.

Excluded from the wedge: scraped-list outreach, sender-identity obfuscation, domain rotation intended to evade reputation controls, deceptive warming, or unsolicited bulk-email infrastructure.

## Direct substitution of the public artifact

The exact no-credential report is already free:

- **Red Sift Investigate** provides a free live-message check of DMARC, SPF, DKIM, BIMI, MTA-STS and TLS in about 30 seconds; it expressly distinguishes live signing from static DNS lookup.
  https://redsift.com/tools/investigate
- **Inboxproof** offers a no-account, free domain audit with seven checks, findings and suggested next steps. Its own limitation statement correctly says a good configuration score does not guarantee inbox delivery.
  https://inboxproof.email/
- **SPFWise** offers unlimited free domain scans, full grades, findings and exact records to fix; paid value is continuous monitoring, DMARC aggregate-report parsing, alerts and white-label reporting.
  https://spfwise.com/en
- Buyers themselves name established diagnostics such as Google Postmaster Tools and MXToolbox as expected verification methods.

A Waterfound DNS scanner or PDF report would duplicate existing zero-price, no-account outputs.

## Acceptance boundary

The paid jobs require predicates public DNS cannot establish:

- which sending services are actually authorized and still in use;
- whether every provider signs real mail with the intended DKIM selector;
- whether SPF/DKIM aligns on actual messages;
- sender and IP reputation inside Google, Microsoft and enterprise filters;
- DMARC aggregate/forensic report interpretation over time;
- bounce, complaint, unsubscribe and suppression data;
- inbox placement across real providers;
- safe migration from monitoring to quarantine/reject;
- DNS/provider changes and rollback;
- campaign content, segmentation and list hygiene;
- before/after delivery evidence.

Even a syntactically perfect domain can reach spam, and an unknown DKIM selector cannot be exhaustively inferred from DNS. Claiming deliverability from public records alone would be misleading.
